# MedQA 2026-05-04 — Wrong-letter independence test (overturned claim)

Generated by `scripts/wrong_letter_independence.py`.


## Retracted public claim

On 2026-05-04 our preliminary YC narrative reported:
> *When both arms are wrong on the same question, they pick the same wrong letter 11/32 = 34.4% of the time — H₀ for independent uniform errors predicts 33.3% (p = 1.00); Asha's errors are statistically independent of Gemini's parametric priors.*

This claim is **incorrect**. The denominator (the b+d=32 'both wrong' McNemar cell) includes cases where Gemini's response was unparseable — cases where Gemini did not emit any letter and therefore cannot 'share a wrong letter' with Asha.

## Correctly-conditioned test

Of the 32 both-wrong questions:
- 12 have BOTH arms emitting a parseable wrong letter
- 20 have Asha parsed-wrong + Gemini UNPARSEABLE
- 0 have Asha UNPARSEABLE + Gemini parsed-wrong
- 0 have both unparseable

On the valid sub-test (n=12):
- Same-letter agreement: **11/12 = 91.7%**
- Wilson 95% CI: [64.6%, 98.5%]
- H₀ (independent random pick among the 3 wrong letters): 1/3 ≈ 33.3%
- Two-sided exact binomial p-value: **4.704e-05**

**Verdict: REJECT H₀.** When both Asha and Gemini emit a parseable wrong letter, they pick the SAME wrong letter in 11/12 cases. The errors are strongly correlated, NOT independent.

## Original (misconditioned) calculation, for traceability

On the original n=d=32 denominator (which includes Gemini-unparseable cases that can never share a letter), the rate is 11/32 = 34.4%. This is the number we originally reported. It is arithmetically correct but the choice of denominator was wrong, which inverted the conclusion.

## What this means for the architecture story

Asha's +3.22 pp paired lift over bare Gemini is **not** explained by Asha generating reasoning paths that are statistically independent of Gemini's parametric priors. The lift is explained by:

1. **Format-compliance rescue (META_CORRECT primitive)**: of the 66 questions where Asha is right and Gemini is wrong, ~51 are cases where Gemini's response was unparseable and Asha's synthesis layer recovered a structured answer.
2. **Retrieval-grounded substitution**: on the remaining questions, KIL evidence steers Asha toward the correct letter even when bare Gemini would have picked a wrong one.

When Asha CANNOT rescue and both arms commit to a parseable wrong letter, the synthesis layer almost always inherits Gemini's parametric prior (same wrong letter 11/12). This is honest, useful information about how the architecture works.
